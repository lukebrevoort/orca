Orca / naming room

A quieter name for human mail.

A short exploration for the product that is becoming a calm workplace for people, conversations, and the signal between them.

Working recommendation

Start with Tideline.

It suggests a clear boundary between signal and noise, keeps the quiet coastal feeling from Orca, and gives the inbox a useful metaphor: the important things reach the line first.

Shortlist 1. Tideline 2. Current 3. Morrow Keep Orca as the codename

What the name needs to carry

human signal calm reading easy to say room to grow ownable enough to explore

The candidates

Tideline

A boundary where the signal arrives. Calm, directional, and naturally connected to the idea of an inbox.

Best fit for the current product promise; a little more distinctive than Orca.
Current

Something live and moving, without noise. It gives the product energy while staying grounded in flow.

Strong voice, though it can sound slightly technical or infrastructure-adjacent.
Morrow

A gentle promise of what comes next. It fits Later, reminders, and a product that helps people return at the right time.

Warm and memorable; the meaning needs a little introduction.
Kith

A word for familiar people and company. It makes the human-to-human thesis explicit in a compact name.

Most human-first; pronunciation and recognition may need support.
Lilt

Writing should feel lighter here. It is warm, writerly, and easy to remember.

Friendly and playful, but less obviously about attention or email.
Still

Quiet, present, and readable. A simple name for a product that makes room for the thought.

Elegant, but likely the most crowded and difficult to own.

A useful next decision

01Say Tideline out loud.
02Test Current in the UI.
03Compare Morrow’s warmth.
04Keep Orca if the symbol wins.

These are creative directions, not clearance results. Before committing, check trademarks, domains, app-store names, and the names of adjacent communication products.
